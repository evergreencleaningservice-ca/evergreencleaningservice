# Jan-Pro vs Evergreen: Website, SEO & PPC Report

2026-10-28 · Paolo Leone

## Bottom line

Evergreen now has a better-built website than Jan-Pro, and it is losing anyway. The 15 Oct rebuild gave it cleaner crawl, canonical and schema hygiene (technical 84/100 vs Jan-Pro's junk-filled 3,600-URL estate; LCP is slower and security headers are missing), richer local schema, a clearer mobile fold and faster pages than Jan-Pro Canada. None of that is visible in search yet: on real Google results for Canada across eight commercial queries Evergreen appeared in no local pack, no AI Overview and no organic top ten (two headless sessions, location undetermined; a Toronto rank grid and Search Console replace this in month one), Google still shows the pre-rebuild titles for its office and commercial pages, its Google Ads have not shown since 17 Sep 2026 per the Ads Transparency Center, and nobody can prove where any lead comes from because the container holds Google Ads only.

Jan-Pro wins on three things Evergreen can copy and one it cannot. It can copy proof (a guarantee, a named inspection system, numbers beside every button), review volume (786 Google reviews across three GTA profiles against Evergreen's 10), and measurement (GA4, Bing, Meta, LinkedIn, 90-day attribution, leads split by type). It cannot copy a national brand with 2,930 posts and a domain from 2000; it should not try.

Two client decisions on 22 Oct moved against the plan: the quote forms went back to 8 required fields with a street address, and a 2.5 MB AI-avatar video bubble, still carrying a HeyGen watermark, was added that covers the phone field on phones. Both need a conversation with the client before anything else is built.

**The five moves that matter most**

1. Make the funnel measurable in the first 30 days: account access, GA4, GTM triggers for the five events the site already pushes, Ads conversion check, offline-conversion uploads; and switch off the Google tag's automatic email hashing until a consent position exists. No other result can be judged before this exists.
2. Fix conversion today: 4-field form with an invisible captcha, remove the mobile chat bubble, one reply promise everywhere, then restart Google Ads with the 2-hour reply as the headline.
3. Build proof: 4–6 Google reviews a month, real photos, a rebuilt /reviews/ and /about-us/ with named people, a written guarantee and inspection routine, the first case study.
4. Fix the entity and win locally: one business name everywhere with "formerly" and alternateName, GBP completed (categories, photos, posts, premises confirmed), five directory listings corrected, the .org name-twin resolved.
5. Build only pages the SERPs reward: a Toronto cost guide, four specialty service pages (floor care, day porter, carpet, window), four industry pages (warehouses, retail, schools, dental-only), and three enriched location pages; prune the blog to ~22 posts with dates, authors and service links.

Expected effect, stated as what to measure rather than a forecast: leads attributable by source within 30 days; form completion rate up after the form change; a profile with 20+ reviews, correct hours and weekly posts by day 90 (pack entry is checked on the rank grid, not promised); first-page impressions for the new service and industry pages within one quarter of publishing. Spend, traffic and ranking baselines are not known from here and are not estimated.

**Fix now (costs leads today)**

- On 22 Oct 2026 the quote forms were restored to the original site's field set (commit 5246207, "bring back the original site's fields and notification email"): 8 required fields including street address, city and postal code, with a visible reCAPTCHA checkbox, on all 25 forms including the paid pages. Jan-Pro asks for 6 fields and no address. The component header records it as the client's own request, with the conversion trade-off accepted. It undoes the 15 Oct change and is the largest single conversion risk on the site; it deserves a second conversation with the client once GA4 can show the form-start-to-lead rate.
- The chat avatar covers the Phone field on [/request-a-quote/](https://www.evergreencleaningservice.ca/request-a-quote/), hero content and body text on mobile. Its close button is 26×26 px at 3.64:1 contrast and it sometimes renders as a black circle. The clip itself is an AI-avatar video that still carries a HeyGen free-tier watermark, loaded on all 73 indexable pages; a buyer who recognises the mark learns the "account executive" speaking to them is synthetic. Remove it on mobile or move it clear, and replace the clip with a real person or drop it.
- The quote form's top error banner tells visitors to tick "I'm not a robot" when no such box is on screen yet. The four address sub-fields are required but carry no asterisk (only the group label does), so a buyer who fills every starred field still gets three errors plus the captcha nag. The submit handler refuses to post if the browser blocks reCAPTCHA (Brave shields, uBlock, some corporate proxies), an untested loss path. Eight required fields is high for a first contact, and the general contact form requires all ten of its fields, including a full postal address, just to send a message.
- The eight service checkboxes do not match the site's service names: no Industrial & Warehouse; post-construction is labelled "Reno Construction Clean-up". There is no facility size, type or frequency field, so every lead needs a qualifying call.
- In-page confirmation says "as soon as possible"; [/thank-you/](https://www.evergreencleaningservice.ca/thank-you/) says "within 2 hours". Pick one and match the ads.
- [/reviews/](https://www.evergreencleaningservice.ca/reviews/) is a testimonial submission form with the blog sidebar and a tag cloud (including Covid-19). It shows no reviews.
- Location pages such as [/locations/toronto-downtown/](https://www.evergreencleaningservice.ca/locations/toronto-downtown/) open with an all-caps title-tag H1 ("… | Evergreen Office Cleaning"), a large gap, and no CTA or phone in the fold.
- At 320 px the header "Get a quote" button disappears.
- The word "guarantee" appears nowhere on the home, office, quote or paid pages, and inspections are described only as "inspections and adjustments". Jan-Pro's H1 ends "Guaranteed." and its Canadian site states the remedy (issues resolved within one business day, otherwise a complimentary clean). This needs the client's wording (intake B2).

**Improve next**

- Add a site-wide fixed bottom Call + Quote bar on mobile (Jan-Pro's strongest mobile pattern).
- Raise mobile body text to 16 px; footer and area links (26–28 px tall), social icons (24 px), "Read more" (22 px) and breadcrumbs (15 px) to 44 px tap targets.
- Replace AI-looking hero, card and blog imagery with the client's own crew and site photos; export the logo with a transparent background.
- The homepage "Google Reviews" block is a static snapshot, not a live widget, so new reviews never appear and the "5 years ago" labels cannot change without a rebuild. Add named, dated, recent reviews next to every form, pulled from the live profile, and company names on testimonials.
- The honeypot field returns a fake success, so a DOM-filling assistant loses the lead silently; return a distinct but harmless state instead.
- The legacy blog sidebar, with a search box, Recent Posts and a tag cloud that still lists Coronavirus and Covid-19, sits on 49 of 73 pages, including About, Contact, Reviews and Privacy. Keep it to the blog, or drop the tag cloud.

**Not measured:** Jan-Pro's mobile menu and quote form did not render in headless Chromium, so its form UX is judged from markup only. Screenshots (60 PNGs) are in the audit folder.
