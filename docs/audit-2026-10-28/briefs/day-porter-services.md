Commercial-transactional with a definitional layer. Property managers of multi-tenant and condo buildings, office managers in busy headquarters, clinic and dealership managers. They are asking "what is a day porter and do we need one in addition to night cleaning?", "what does the porter do during the day?", "how many hours?" and "what does it cost?". Google rewards a **service page that opens with a clear definition** (JAN-PRO, Intergroup and Pro-Clean all do), a duties list, a porter-vs-janitorial comparison and FAQ. Knowledge level: low to moderate; the term is trade jargon.

### Content Gaps and Opportunities

**Topic gaps**
- **How many hours do we need?** A simple sizing guide (by lobby traffic, washroom count, tenant count) with three coverage patterns: morning-only, split shift, full day. Pro-Clean has one FAQ sentence; nobody has a table.
- **A sample porter day** — a timeline from 7:30 opening checks to 4:30 close-down. Nobody shows it, and it is the clearest way to explain the service.
- **Winter entrances in Toronto**: salt, slush and mat rotation November to March; the single most local reason buildings add daytime coverage. Nobody says it.
- **How porter and night crew hand off** (shared log, who restocks, who reports maintenance issues). Pro-Clean has a paragraph; make it concrete.

**Depth gaps**
- Duties lists are nouns. Add frequency ("washroom checks every 60–90 minutes in a busy lobby", "entrance glass twice daily") as typical, set in the proposal.
- Porter vs janitorial comparison exists (Pro-Clean, JAN-PRO FAQ); add a third column: "both together", which is what most buildings actually buy.
- Pricing: only Pro-Clean lists factors. Explain hourly or daily coverage pricing vs the monthly janitorial contract, and that the two can be one invoice.

**Quality gaps**
- Three of six pages are thin, broken or have no alt text or meta description. A tidy 1,300-word page with real structure outperforms them on quality alone.
- No competitor has a day-porter-specific review or photo. Evergreen cannot invent one either; use a general review and a real photo once B5 is done.

**Gap priority:**
1. Coverage-hours sizing table — high impact, unique, low effort.
2. Sample porter day timeline — high impact, unique, low effort.
3. Porter / night crew / both comparison table — high impact, low effort.
4. Winter entrance paragraph — medium impact, unique, low effort.
5. Hand-off and reporting section — medium; depends on confirming how Evergreen would run it.
