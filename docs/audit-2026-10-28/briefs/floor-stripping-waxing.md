### Search Intent

Transactional with a strong informational layer. Buyers are facility and property managers with a dull, yellowed or heel-marked VCT floor, often before an inspection, tenant turnover or reopening. They need to (a) confirm the floor needs a strip rather than a recoat, (b) understand cost per square foot and downtime, and (c) book an after-hours job. Google rewards a **service page with a process section, price framing and FAQs**; the top Toronto pages all carry FAQPage markup and price or frequency guidance. Knowledge level: moderate — they know the words "strip and wax" but not "neutralize", "scrub and recoat" or "burnish".

### Content Gaps and Opportunities

**Topic gaps**
- **A "strip, recoat or burnish?" decision guide** with the visual signs for each. Profound has it in prose; nobody has a table a manager can use on a site walk.
- **Downtime planning**: how many hours a floor is out of service, phasing a floor in sections, when furniture goes back, when carts and forklifts can roll. Only Profound touches it ("walkable by morning").
- **Warehouse and safety-line floors**: strip and wax where painted aisle lines must stay visible. Nobody covers it. Evergreen has a draft 50,000 sq ft warehouse case study (B4) that fits exactly.
- **Slip resistance explained for a non-specialist**: what to ask for (the finish manufacturer's slip-resistance data; UL-certified finishes are tested to ASTM D2047). CMG has a paragraph; nobody tells the buyer what document to request.
- **Which floors are not stripped** (hardwood, unsealed stone, rubber, LVT with factory coating). CMG only.

**Depth gaps**
- Process steps exist everywhere but without the two details that separate a good job from a failed one: dwell time and the neutralizing rinse. Say why each matters in one sentence each.
- Frequency: competitors give one range ("every 12–18 months"). Give a cadence table by traffic level, with burnish and scrub-and-recoat in between, and tie it to the building-maintenance programme page.
- Pricing: only Profound publishes ranges. Evergreen should at least publish the **factors** and the unit (per square foot, with a job minimum) — numbers only if the client supplies them.

**Quality gaps**
- No competitor shows a real before/after photo of a GTA floor with a date. Evergreen has one candidate image (warehouse, approval pending B4/B5).
- floorwaxing.ca and ClearView are thin or broken; CMG is bloated. A 1,600–1,800-word page that is tightly structured beats both.

**Gap priority:**
1. Decision table (strip / recoat / burnish) — high impact, unique, low effort.
2. Downtime and phasing section — high impact, unique, low effort.
3. Cadence table by traffic — medium-high, low effort.
4. Before/after photo with case-study link — high impact, blocked on B4/B5.
5. Price factors with unit and minimum — medium; numbers depend on client.

### Unique Angle and Information Gain

Three things no current Toronto page provides together: (1) a **sign-on-the-floor decision table** that tells a manager whether they need a full strip, a scrub-and-recoat or a burnish before they spend money; (2) a **downtime and phasing plan**, including the one warehouse-specific issue (painted safety lines) that logistics operators actually ask about; (3) a **dated, real before/after from a ~50,000 sq ft GTA warehouse** once the client approves the case study. The page should also be explicit that it will recommend the cheaper option when the floor only needs a recoat — the single most trust-building sentence in Profound's page, which Evergreen can say honestly.
