### Content Gaps and Opportunities

**Topic gaps**
- **What the night crew needs from the site**: dock door or man-door access, alarm and sign-in, which zones are off-limits during picking, where the scrubber fills and dumps water, parking for the van. Nobody has this. It is the first conversation an ops manager has with a new contractor.
- **Front office plus warehouse under one contract** — Profound names "front offices"; nobody explains that the same contractor can run nightly office cleaning and weekly warehouse floors on one proposal, which is Evergreen's natural strength (office-cleaning heritage).
- **What is not included** stated plainly: production machinery interiors, hazardous or regulated waste, racking repairs, pest control, confined-space work. Pro-Clean has two FAQ lines; make it a section. This also protects the client (A8/A10 pattern).
- **Dust control as a safety topic**: visibility of labels and aisle lines, fire-load from combustible dust, slip hazards at docks in winter (salt and slush tracked in by forklifts) — a genuinely Toronto-specific detail nobody mentions.

**Depth gaps**
- Scope by zone exists (Profound) but with no frequency. Give a zone / task / typical frequency table.
- Compliance: CMG cites OHSA O. Reg. 851; nobody explains what the housekeeping requirement actually means for a cleaning contract (floors kept free of obstructions, hazards and accumulations). One accurate paragraph with a link to the regulation beats a badge.
- Pricing: Pro-Clean lists factors; nobody explains the difference between a per-visit floor-scrub price and a monthly janitorial price for the staff areas.

**Quality gaps**
- No competitor shows a dated, real GTA warehouse photo with scale. Evergreen has the ~50,000 sq ft candidate (B4).
- JAN-PRO's compliance language is American (OSHA); Evergreen writes Ontario (OHSA, WSIB, WHMIS).
- MCA's page is heavy (99 images) and slow; a lean page with the same substance will out-perform it on experience.

**Gap priority:**
1. Zone / task / frequency table — high impact, low effort.
2. "Working on a live site" section with the access checklist — high impact, unique, low effort.
3. "What is not included" section — medium-high impact, protects the client, low effort.
4. Case study card and photo — high impact, blocked on B4.
5. Ontario compliance paragraph — medium impact, low effort, must be accurate.

### Unique Angle and Information Gain

The page treats a warehouse as an **operation, not a floor**: a zone / task / frequency table, a live-site access checklist, and a plain "not included" list give an ops manager the three things they need to brief their GM and compare quotes. It pairs this with Evergreen's real advantage — the same contractor runs the nightly office clean and the weekly floor programme on one proposal with one contact — and, once approved, the only dated, real ~50,000 sq ft GTA before/after in the set. Ontario-specific compliance wording (OHSA O. Reg. 851, WSIB, WHMIS) replaces the American boilerplate on the franchise pages.
