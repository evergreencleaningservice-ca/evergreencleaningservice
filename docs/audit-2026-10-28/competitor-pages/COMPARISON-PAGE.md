### 2.2 How a commercial cleaning franchise is structured (320–360 words)
Explain factually, using JAN-PRO Canada's own description as the example:
- JAN-PRO Canada runs a three-tier model: JAN-PRO Canada is the national franchisor; each region is independently owned and led by a regional developer that is itself a franchisee of JAN-PRO Canada and a franchisor to the local "Certified Business Owners" (CBOs, or unit franchisees). Source: https://jan-pro.ca/en/how-jan-pro-canada-works/ — quote: *"Each regional office is a franchisee of JAN-PRO Canada, while also acting as a franchisor to that region's local Certified Business Owners ('CBOs' or 'unit franchisees')."*
- CBOs are *"100% independent owners and operators"*, *"hire their own staff, and lead the relationships with their own customers"*; JAN-PRO states *"No subcontracting—CBOs and their staff perform the work."* (same URL).
- Every jan-pro.ca page footer: *"Cleaning services provided by independently owned and operated JAN-PRO unit franchisees."* (e.g. https://jan-pro.ca/en/office/toronto-west/).
- The US franchising page describes the franchisee's side of the arrangement: the brand *"finds all the customers for you"*, *"you don't have to do any selling"*, and plans *"can take as little as three hours per week"*; franchise purchasers are background-checked and complete a *"five-course certification program"*. Source: https://jan-pro.com/franchising/ (US page; Canadian terms may differ — say so).
- Say plainly what this means for a buyer: the person with the mop is normally a small-business owner (or that owner's employee) operating under licence, not an employee of the brand or of the regional office. The brand supplies systems, certification, inspection and the guarantee; the CBO supplies the labour and holds their own insurance/WSIB account.
- Name the other franchise brands active in the GTA factually and briefly: Coverall (franchise network; says it serves Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton, Vaughan, Markham — surfaced in search results, verify on coverall.com before publishing), Anago (master-franchise model; Toronto-area master office in Mississauga per search results — verify on anagocleaning.com), ServiceMaster Clean (franchise since 1952 per its own franchise blog — verify). ### 2.6 Nine questions to ask any commercial cleaner before you sign (380–420 words)
1. Who exactly will be in my building — employees of the company I'm contracting with, a franchisee, or a subcontractor? (JAN-PRO's answer is on its "how it works" page; ask the same of every bidder.)
2. Whose WSIB account covers them? Ask for a current WSIB clearance certificate in the name of the entity whose staff enter the building.
3. Whose liability insurance and bond? Ask for a certificate of insurance naming you as additional insured.
4. Who do I call at 7 a.m. when something is wrong, and can that person change the scope?
5. Is the scope written, task by task, with frequencies? (Both JAN-PRO and Evergreen say they provide a written scope after a walkthrough — sources in table.)
6. Will it be the same people every visit, and what happens when one is sick?
7. What is the guarantee, in writing, and what triggers it?
8. What products are used, and can I see the safety data sheets and any third-party certification?
9. What is the notice period and how are price changes handled?
Internal link: [request a quote](/request-a-quote/) once, at the end of this section.

## 4. FAQ block (8 Q&As; mirror in FAQPage schema)

1. **Is JAN-PRO a franchise, and who actually cleans my office?** Yes. JAN-PRO Canada describes a three-tier franchise: JAN-PRO Canada franchises regional offices, which in turn franchise local Certified Business Owners; JAN-PRO states that CBOs and their own staff do the cleaning and that there is no subcontracting. Every jan-pro.ca page notes services are "provided by independently owned and operated JAN-PRO unit franchisees." (Sources: how-jan-pro-canada-works; toronto-west.)
2. **What is the difference between a franchise cleaning company and a local company?** In a franchise, a brand licenses systems, training and a guarantee to independent operators who do the work and hold their own insurance and WSIB; in a local company, one business employs the crew, holds the WSIB account and insurance, and the owner is the final decision-maker. Neither is automatically better; the structure decides who you call and whose paperwork you check.
3. **Does it matter whether my cleaners are employees or franchisees?** For the floor, not necessarily; for risk, yes. Ask whose WSIB clearance certificate and certificate of insurance cover the people in your building, and get them in that entity's name.
4. **How much does office cleaning cost in Toronto?** Nobody publishes Toronto rate cards. JAN-PRO Canada publishes a national starting point of $250 a month (as of 2026-10-28); Evergreen quotes after a free walkthrough. Price moves with square footage and layout, frequency, scope, facility type, hours and consumables — compare quotes on identical scope and frequency.
5. **What should I ask for before signing a commercial cleaning contract?** A written task-and-frequency scope, a WSIB clearance certificate, a certificate of insurance naming you, the name and role of your contact, the staffing plan for absences, the guarantee in writing, product SDSs, and the notice and price-change terms.
6. **Can a one-office local company cover several GTA sites?** Evergreen dispatches crews from one Toronto office to Toronto, Peel, Halton, York and Durham; a franchise network such as JAN-PRO offers national accounts with centralized billing across provinces. Match the vendor to your footprint.
7. **Do franchise cleaners offer a guarantee, and what is Evergreen's?** JAN-PRO publishes a 100% satisfaction guarantee: scheduled services completed on time, issues resolved within one business day, otherwise a complimentary cleaning. Evergreen's commitment is a written scope, inspections and a dedicated account executive — `REPLACE_EVERGREEN_GUARANTEE_TEXT` once the owner adopts a written guarantee.
8. **Why is a cleaning company publishing this comparison?** Because buyers ask us these questions. We are a competitor of the brands named; every statement about them is quoted from their public pages with the date we read it, and we will correct anything they tell us is out of date.
