## Where Germs Collect in an Office

Germs spread in an office the same way people work in one: through the things everyone touches. A cold or a stomach bug moves from hand to surface to hand, so the places that need the most attention are not always the ones that look dirty. They are the ones many people handle every day.

**At a glance:**

- Reception and lobby
- Washrooms
- Photocopiers and printers
- Coffee makers, water coolers and fridges
- Keypads and door handles
- Boardrooms
- Blinds and windowsills
- Hallways and stairways
- Desks and work areas

Most of these are easy to miss in a routine clean, because they are small, they belong to everyone, or they sit out of the way.

![Cleaner wiping office desks in a Toronto office](/images/spring-office-desk-cleaning-toronto.jpg)

### Reception and Lobby

The reception area is the first thing visitors see and the place they touch most: the front door, the desk, the pen, the chairs. Everything the public handles should be cleaned and disinfected every visit, and the floor here takes more wear than anywhere else, so it is worth a machine scrub or refinish every few months.

### Washrooms

Washrooms collect more bacteria than any other room in the building. Public and staff washrooms both need cleaning and sanitizing on every visit, with taps, flush handles, dispensers and door handles disinfected, and a periodic deep clean of floors, grout and partitions.

### Photocopiers and Printers

Buttons, screens, paper drawers and lids are handled by many people a day and almost never cleaned. Wipe them daily with a product the manufacturer allows.

### Coffee Makers, Water Coolers and Fridges

Anything in the kitchen that serves food or drink is handled all day. Coffee-maker handles, water-cooler buttons and dispensers, and fridge and microwave handles should be wiped and disinfected at least daily. Water coolers in particular are easy to forget, because they usually sit in a corridor rather than the kitchen.

### Keypads and Door Handles

Washroom doors get attention; the rest often do not. Every door handle and push plate, keypads and fingerprint readers, and the handles on cupboards, drawers and closets are touched as often. The building's exterior handles are touched by visitors and delivery drivers as well.

![Cleaner disinfecting an office door handle](/images/touchpoint-disinfection-door-handle.jpg)

### Boardrooms

Boardrooms are used by staff and visitors all day. The table, chairs, phones, remote controls and conferencing equipment should be wiped after each meeting if possible, and at least once a day.

### Blinds and Windowsills

Dust settles on blinds and sills and is rarely touched until it is visible. Have them cleaned on a schedule, monthly or quarterly depending on the building, rather than when someone notices.

### Hallways and Stairways

Handrails and the push bars on stairwell doors take a lot of hands. Floors should be cleaned daily and handrails sanitized daily.

### Desks and Work Areas

Keyboards, mice, phones and chair arms are touched constantly by the person at the desk, and by anyone who shares it. Encourage staff to keep desks clear so cleaners can reach the surface, give them somewhere other than their desk to eat lunch, and make sure your cleaning scope says whether desks are wiped and how often.

![Industrial scrubber cleaning an office corridor](/images/night-janitorial-crew-office-toronto.jpg)

### Setting a Schedule

None of this needs to happen all at once. The high-touch points above belong in every routine visit; floors, blinds and a deep clean of washrooms and kitchens can be planned through the year. A quieter week, such as the end of summer or the holidays, is a good time for the deeper work, because there are fewer people in the way.
