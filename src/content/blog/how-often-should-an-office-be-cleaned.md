<h2 id="recommended-cleaning-frequencies">How Often Should an Office Be Cleaned?</h2>

An office used every weekday should generally receive cleaning on each occupied day in its busiest shared areas. Professional whole-office visits can range from once or twice weekly in a lightly used suite to five nights a week in a busy workplace. Daily upkeep still needs an assigned person when the cleaning crew is absent.

The suggested starting points below are Evergreen's planning examples, not mandatory standards or staffing formulas. Review the space with your [office cleaning provider](/services/office-cleaning/) before deciding which tasks require each visit.

<h2 id="daily-weekly-periodic-cleaning">Which Office Areas Need Daily, Weekly or Periodic Cleaning?</h2>

Washrooms, kitchens, food waste and busy touch points generally need attention every occupied day; quieter surfaces and detailed work can follow a weekly or periodic rotation. Adjust immediately for spills, visible soil or unusually heavy use.

### Washrooms and kitchens: assess use throughout the day

Check washrooms during occupied hours and after busy periods, looking at fixtures, waste, floors, soap and paper supplies. CCOHS's [Workplace Housekeeping — Basic Guide](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/hsprograms/housekeeping/house.html), consulted in November 2026, advises washroom cleaning once or more each shift. A nightly visit may need daytime support where use is heavy.

Ontario's [washroom maintenance and cleaning-record guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/maintaining-clean-washroom-facilities-workers), current when checked in November 2026, explains employers' duties to maintain clean, sanitary worker washrooms and make cleaning records available. Its posted-record route includes dates and times of the two most recent cleanings, either near the washroom or electronically with access directions. Include responsibility for those records in the scope.

For kitchens, inspect after lunch and before closing. Address spills promptly and identify who handles dishes, food disposal and appliance interiors; general counter cleaning does not settle those responsibilities.

### Routine cleaning, deep cleaning and disinfection

Routine cleaning handles recurring soil and waste; deep cleaning addresses detailed buildup beyond the regular visit. Examples include carpet extraction, high dusting, floor edges and agreed appliance interiors. Put those tasks on a separate calendar after inspecting their condition, rather than assuming every office needs the same monthly treatment.

Disinfection is a separate, product-specific process. The Public Health Agency of Canada's [cleaning and disinfecting guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/respiratory-infectious-diseases-reduce-spread-personal-protective-measures/cleaning-disinfecting.html), consulted in November 2026, recommends cleaning first and following product-label directions, including contact time. Agree where disinfection is required; a deep-cleaning label alone does not define it.

<h2 id="size-occupancy-hybrid-work">How Does Office Size and Employee Count Affect Cleaning Frequency?</h2>

Office size determines how much space there is to clean, while actual attendance helps determine how quickly it becomes soiled. Build an office cleaning schedule around both, then account for visitors and shared facilities.

Small offices still need daily washroom and kitchen attention when occupied. Medium offices often concentrate demand around lunch breaks and meeting rooms. Large offices may need zones, with reception and shared facilities serviced more often than quiet rooms.

Floor materials change the work, too. Carpet needs vacuuming and condition-based extraction, while tile and finished hard floors need compatible cleaning methods. Toronto winter grit can justify extra entrance attention without increasing every task across the building.

### Match hybrid attendance and operating hours

Hybrid work changes which days need the most attention, not just the weekly visit total. If most employees attend Tuesday through Thursday, place full visits after those days and arrange essential upkeep on quieter occupied days. Shared desks need cleaning when users change, even if total attendance is low.

Record peak daily headcounts rather than dividing attendance evenly across the week. Also allow access and drying time after closing; multiple shifts may require cleaning between shifts or during agreed breaks.

<h2 id="example-cleaning-schedule">Example Cleaning Schedule for a 6,000-Square-Foot Toronto Office</h2>

For an illustrative 6,000-square-foot office with 40 employees, two washrooms, one shared kitchen and five-day occupancy, start with professional cleaning after each weekday. This is a planning example, not a customer story or a quote; a walkthrough determines the final scope.

<div class="guide-panel">
<p><strong>Adaptable office cleaning schedule</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Monday–Friday after hours:</strong> clean both washrooms and the kitchen, empty agreed bins, clean shared touch points and meeting tables, vacuum busy routes and clean soiled hard floors.</li>
<li><strong>During each occupied day:</strong> an assigned staff member checks supplies, food waste and washrooms after lunch; arrange additional cleaning when checks identify a need.</li>
<li><strong>Once weekly:</strong> dust cleared desks and accessible ledges, complete a full carpet vacuum, detail floor edges and clean agreed appliance interiors.</li>
<li><strong>Monthly inspection:</strong> review entrance mats, carpet spots, high ledges and floor condition; book detailed work when indicated.</li>
<li><strong>Periodic planned work:</strong> schedule extraction, high dusting or suitable floor care separately, with access and drying time agreed.</li>

</div>

Use a checklist with the task, responsible person, planned frequency, completion date and notes. Review it after the first month and after occupancy changes. Keep weather-related entrance care on the plan alongside this routine.

<h2 id="common-scheduling-mistakes">Common Mistakes When Planning an Office Cleaning Schedule</h2>

The common mistake is buying a visit frequency without defining what happens during and between visits. Avoid these gaps:

- **Using floor area alone:** record headcount, visitors, washroom fixtures and meal use as well.
- **Reducing every task on quiet days:** inspect shared facilities before cutting essential work.
- **Leaving responsibility unclear:** name who handles dishes, supplies, spills and daytime checks.
- **Treating deep cleaning as automatic:** list detailed tasks and their booking process separately.
- **Keeping an outdated scope:** revise it after hiring, layout changes, events or extended hours.

Before signing, ask:

1. Which rooms and tasks are included on each visit, and which rotate?
2. Who replenishes supplies and maintains washroom cleaning records?
3. Who handles urgent daytime issues and missed tasks?
4. Are the same crew and one contact assigned to the office?
5. How are extra work, access arrangements and schedule changes approved?

<details>
<summary><h3>Does every office need professional cleaning daily?</h3></summary>
<p>No, whole-office professional cleaning is not necessary every day in every workplace. A lightly occupied suite may suit fewer visits if someone handles essential cleaning between them. Washrooms, kitchen surfaces, food waste and spills still need attention on occupied days. Confirm responsibilities during a walkthrough and review whether the arrangement keeps each area clean.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><h3>Is weekly cleaning enough for a small office?</h3></summary>
<p>Weekly professional cleaning can be a starting point for a small, lightly used office with reliable upkeep between visits. It should not mean leaving washrooms, kitchen mess or food waste untouched all week. Review daily attendance, visitors and facility use, then increase visits if checks show that soil or waste is accumulating.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><h3>How should hybrid offices schedule cleaning?</h3></summary>
<p>Schedule the most complete visits after the busiest attendance days, while maintaining essential cleaning whenever the office is occupied. Use actual bookings or headcounts to identify peaks. Include shared desks and meeting rooms in the plan, assign between-visit duties and review the arrangement when team attendance patterns change.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><h3>How often should office washrooms be cleaned?</h3></summary>
<p>Plan to clean occupied office washrooms at least each workday, with more attention when use or inspections require it. CCOHS advises once or more each shift. Check fixtures, floors, bins and supplies during the day. In Ontario, also assign responsibility for making worker washroom cleaning records available under the applicable requirements.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><h3>How often does an office need deep cleaning?</h3></summary>
<p>Deep-cleaning intervals should follow the condition of the surfaces and the tasks involved. Inspect carpets, high ledges, appliance interiors and floor finishes, then set a separate plan for work beyond regular visits. Monthly inspections can help identify needs, but they do not mean every detailed task must be performed monthly.</p>
</details>

<summary><h3>Can office cleaning take place on weekends?</h3></summary>

A weekend visit does not replace essential weekday upkeep in a busy office. Agree on building access, alarms and responsibilities before the crew arrives.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><h3>When should an office adjust its cleaning frequency?</h3></summary>
<p>Adjust the frequency when attendance, visitors, operating hours or visible conditions change. Repeated full bins, soiled washrooms or grit remaining at entrances are reasons to review the scope promptly. Identify the affected tasks first, then agree on additional attention and update the written checklist so responsibilities remain clear.</p>
