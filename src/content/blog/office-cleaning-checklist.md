<h2 id="how-to-use-this-checklist">How to Use This Office Cleaning Checklist</h2>

Print it, walk the office with it, and cross out what does not apply. A ten-person suite with one washroom does not need the same list as a floor of a downtown tower, and the point of a checklist is to fit the building, not to be long. Three decisions shape it:

- **Frequency.** Washrooms, kitchens, bins and busy touch points need attention every day the office is used; most other tasks can rotate weekly or monthly. The [office cleaning frequency guide](/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned/) explains how to set the number of visits.
- **Ownership.** Some tasks belong to the cleaning crew, some to staff between visits, and a few to specialist trades. The "who does what" section below separates them.
- **The written scope.** When a cleaning company quotes an office, the quote should list the tasks and how often each is done. This checklist is the raw material for that list; the [pricing guide](/office-cleaning-cost-toronto-guide/) covers what the list does to the monthly figure.

The seasons add work on top of this routine (salt in winter, pollen in spring); the [seasonal office cleaning checklist](/seasonal-office-cleaning-checklist/) covers that layer.

<h2 id="daily-office-cleaning-tasks">Daily Office Cleaning Tasks</h2>

"Daily" means every day the office is occupied. For a hybrid office that is busy Tuesday to Thursday, it means those days, with a lighter check on the others.

Two things make the daily list work. Desks that are reasonably clear can be wiped; desks under paper cannot, so a desk-wiping task only belongs on the list if staff keep them clear. And bins with liners are emptied in a fraction of the time, so liners are worth their cost.

<h2 id="weekly-office-cleaning-tasks">Weekly Office Cleaning Tasks</h2>

Weekly tasks are the ones that keep the office looking cared for rather than merely tidy. On a nightly schedule they are spread across the week; on a two- or three-visit schedule they are grouped onto one visit.

- Vacuum all carpeted areas edge to edge, including under desks where they are accessible, and the meeting rooms.
- Damp-mop all hard floors, including the areas away from the main walkways.
- Dust desks that are clear, filing cabinets, window sills, ledges and the tops of partitions.
- Clean the interior glass on doors and partitions.
- Wipe phones, keyboards and monitors where the scope includes them (see "who does what").
- Clean the inside of the refrigerator of spills and wipe the shelves; staff clear out old food first.
- Wipe the fronts of kitchen cabinets and the dishwasher, and descale the kettle or coffee machine if it is on the scope.
- Clean washroom walls around sinks and urinals, and the undersides of toilet seats and rims.
- Sanitize the bins themselves, not just change the liners.
- Dust and wipe the reception furniture and any plants (artificial ones collect dust fastest).
- Check and restock the first-aid and hygiene supplies if that is part of the arrangement.

<h2 id="room-by-room-checklist">Room-by-Room Office Cleaning Checklist</h2>

The same tasks, arranged the way a crew actually moves through a building. Use this version for the walkthrough.

### Reception and entrance

- Daily: entrance floor and mats, glass at hand height, desk, handles and push plates, seating tidied, bins.
- Weekly: full glass clean, dusting of furniture, ledges and displays, hard floor damp-mopped.
- Periodic: mats replaced or washed, high dusting, floor scrubbed and burnished.

### Workstations and open-plan areas

- Daily: bins, walkway vacuuming, spills, shared touch points.
- Weekly: edge-to-edge vacuuming, clear desks dusted, partition tops, interior glass.
- Periodic: upholstered chairs vacuumed, vents and lights dusted, carpet extraction.

### Private offices and meeting rooms

- Daily: table and chair arms wiped, litter cleared, bins, whiteboard if used.
- Weekly: dusting of furniture and sills, floors fully vacuumed or mopped, glass walls cleaned.
- Periodic: upholstery, blinds, carpet extraction.

### Kitchen and break room

- Daily: counters, tables, sink, microwave, outside of appliances, bins and recycling, floor.
- Weekly: fridge interior wiped, cabinet fronts, dishwasher exterior, descaling where agreed.
- Periodic: deep clean including inside cabinets, behind appliances, grout and tile walls.

### Washrooms

- Daily: fixtures cleaned and disinfected, mirrors, partitions, handles, consumables refilled, bins, floor mopped, faults reported.
- Weekly: walls around fixtures, under seats and rims, bins sanitized, partitions fully wiped.
- Periodic: grout and tile deep clean, vents dusted, floor machine-scrubbed.

### Corridors, stairs and service areas

- Daily: floors, handrails, elevator buttons, lights checked.
- Weekly: baseboards and kick plates in the busiest corridors, fire-door glass.
- Periodic: high dusting, floor scrubbing and burnishing, stairwell walls spot-cleaned.

<h2 id="who-does-what">Who Does What</h2>

A checklist only works if each line has an owner. In most Toronto offices the split looks like this, and the walkthrough is where it is agreed.

- **The cleaning crew** handles everything above that happens after hours: floors, washrooms, kitchens, bins, touch points, dusting of clear surfaces, glass, and the periodic work on a dated schedule. A good scope says which of the weekly and monthly items are included in the contract price and which are quoted separately (carpet extraction and floor refinishing usually are).
- **Staff** keep desks clear enough to wipe, clear out the fridge on a set day, wipe their own keyboard and phone between visits if the scope does not include personal equipment, and report spills and faults the same day. A few [cleaning products worth keeping in the office](/5-cleaning-products-you-should-have-in-your-office/) make that easy.
- **Building management or a trade** usually covers exterior windows, HVAC filters and duct cleaning, pest control, and anything that needs a licence.
- **Whoever is responsible for consumables** (toilet paper, hand soap, paper towel, bin liners) should be named in the scope, because "we thought you were buying them" is the most common gap.

If the list is long, that is normal. What matters is that each line says how often and who, and that the cleaning company's written proposal matches it. <h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<details>
<summary>What should be on a daily office cleaning checklist?</summary>
<p>Every day the office is used: washrooms cleaned and disinfected with consumables refilled, the kitchen counters, sink and microwave wiped and bins emptied, desk-side bins emptied, carpet vacuumed in walkways and busy areas, spills dealt with, and shared touch points such as door handles, light switches and printer panels wiped. Reception glass and the entrance floor are daily in any office that receives visitors.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between daily, weekly and monthly office cleaning?</summary>
<p>Daily tasks are about hygiene and the things people touch or use: washrooms, kitchens, bins, touch points. Weekly tasks are about appearance and thoroughness: full vacuuming and mopping, dusting, interior glass. Monthly and periodic tasks reach the places a routine visit does not: high surfaces, upholstery, carpet extraction, floor refinishing and deep cleans of kitchens and washrooms. The weekly and monthly work is spread across visits on a nightly schedule and grouped on a two- or three-visit schedule.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I adapt the checklist for a small office?</summary>
<p>Start from the number of people and the rooms you have, not from the full list. A small office with one washroom and a kitchenette still needs those two rooms attended on every occupied day, but the rest can usually be covered in one or two visits a week, with monthly tasks grouped into one longer visit. Cross out rooms you do not have and merge the weekly and monthly columns if one visit covers both.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Should the cleaning company or our staff wipe desks and keyboards?</summary>
<p>Either, as long as the scope says which. Crews can wipe desks that are kept clear and, where agreed, keyboards, phones and monitors; they cannot clean a desk that is covered in paper or move personal items. Many offices keep personal equipment with the staff who use it and give the crew the shared surfaces. Whichever you choose, write it into the scope so nothing is assumed.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>How often should office carpets be cleaned?</summary>
<p>Vacuuming is daily in walkways and weekly edge to edge; hot-water extraction is usually twice a year to annually, with entrances and corridors done more often in a Toronto winter because of tracked-in salt. Spot-cleaning spills the same day stops most permanent stains.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Who supplies the washroom and kitchen consumables?</summary>
<p>Either the client or the cleaning company, but it has to be agreed in writing. If the cleaning company supplies toilet paper, soap, paper towel and bin liners, the quote should say whether they are included in the monthly price or billed as used. If the client supplies them, the crew restocks from the client's stores and reports when they run low.</p>
</details>

<p>Yes, and it is the best way to compare them. Give each company the same list with your frequencies, ask them to quote that scope, and compare the written proposals line by line. A quote that is cheaper because it leaves out the weekly glass or the monthly high dusting is not cheaper.
