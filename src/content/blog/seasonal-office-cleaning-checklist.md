## An Office Cleaning Plan for Every Season

Routine cleaning keeps an office presentable day to day. But a Toronto year brings salt and slush, pollen, open windows and closed ones, and each season leaves something behind. Planning a little extra work for each one keeps floors, air and surfaces from wearing down between deep cleans.

**At a glance:**

- Winter: entrances, salt and dry air
- Spring: a deep clean and a declutter
- End of summer: catch up while the office is quiet
- Fall: windows, air and pests

![Wet and muddy entrance mat in winter](/images/winter-entrance-floor-care-slush.jpg)

## Winter: Entrances, Salt and Dry Air

### Prepare the main entrance

The entrance takes the worst of the winter, so set it up before the first snow:

- Secured entrance mats, with extra absorbent mats for the worst days
- Wet-floor signs
- A dedicated mop and bucket for the entrance
- Somewhere for boots, if staff change at work
- Salt for the walkway and parking lot, and a shovel by the door

Then keep a routine: mats dry before people arrive, the floor mopped dry through the day, and mats cleaned and dried at the end of it. That is a lot to add to staff duties; a cleaning crew can take it on daily or weekly.

### Remove salt before it does damage

Salt left on a floor scratches and dulls the finish, and salt left in carpet wears the fibres. Water alone will not lift it. A neutralizing floor cleaner, diluted as the manufacturer directs, dissolves salt residue and the grime with it. Entrances and lobbies need extra scrubbing through the winter, and a scrub and recoat or a full [strip and wax](/services/floor-stripping-waxing/) in the spring.

### Look after the air

Heated air in winter is dry, and with windows shut and fewer people going out at lunch, colds spread more easily. Vacuum with HEPA filtration, wipe high and hard surfaces where dust collects (fan blades, window frames, vents), keep air filters changed, and run a humidifier when the air is very dry. Touch points such as door handles, switches, elevator buttons and washroom fixtures should be disinfected on every visit.

## Spring: A Deep Clean and a Declutter

Spring is the traditional time for the jobs the routine does not reach: carpet extraction, washing interior glass and blinds, cleaning under and behind furniture, and repairing the winter's damage to floors.

It is also the time to clear the clutter that makes cleaning harder. A few habits help:

- **Zones.** Separate work areas from storage, files and supplies, so desks hold only what is used every day.
- **A paper system.** Labelled trays or folders for "today", "this week" and "this month" keep paper off the desk.
- **Drawers.** Dividers stop catch-all drawers filling up; empty them every few weeks.
- **One in, one out.** When something new arrives on a desk or shelf, something old goes.

Clear surfaces can be wiped properly, and a tidy office is quicker to clean.

## End of Summer: Catch Up While It Is Quiet

Late summer, with people on holiday, is the easiest time to deep clean, because there are fewer people in the way. Focus on:

- **Reception and lobby.** The surfaces visitors touch, and a deep clean of the floor, which takes the most traffic.
- **Washrooms.** A deep clean of floors, grout, partitions and fixtures.
- **Shared equipment.** Printers, copiers, keyboards, monitors and phones, plus desktops, drawer fronts, handrails and door handles.
- **The kitchen.** Fridges, microwaves, coffee makers and the cupboards behind them.

![Cleaner washing an office window with a squeegee](/images/summer-office-window-cleaning.jpg)

## Fall: Windows, Air and Pests

Fall cleaning matters as much as spring cleaning:

- **Air.** Once the heating comes on and the windows close, whatever dust built up over the summer recirculates. Deep clean carpets, dust high surfaces and vents, and change filters.
- **Windows.** The days get shorter, so clean glass lets in all the daylight there is.
- **Summer's mess.** Pollen and dirt carried in through open windows and doors over the summer have settled into carpets and corners.
- **Pests.** As the weather cools, insects and mice look for food and shelter indoors. Keep kitchens free of crumbs, empty bins daily and dust behind furniture.

Fall is also the time to get the entrance ready for winter again.
